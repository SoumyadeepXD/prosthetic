1. OVERVIEW & SCOPE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This document records bench troubleshooting observations, hardware verification
findings, and minor engineering decisions established during the physical
commissioning of the VAPA robotic arm stack against the Hardware Connection Guide
Rev 2 and VAPA_Robotic_Arm_Connection_Guide.pdf.


2. HARDWARE FINDINGS & PHYSICAL I2C BUS VERIFICATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[DEC-M01] PCA9685 Output Enable (/OE) Pin Behavior:
- Question: Does tying PCA9685 /OE to GND prevent or disrupt I2C communication?
- Finding: NO. /OE (pin 23 of PCA9685) is strictly an active-LOW gate for the
  16 PWM output stages. When /OE is LOW, servo outputs are enabled; when HIGH,
  outputs are High-Z.
- Decision: /OE has zero effect on the internal I2C logic, register bank, or
  ACK response. Grounding /OE for bench testing is acceptable for manual PWM
  validation. In the final system, /OE is strictly controlled by ESP32 GPIO25
  as an independent safety interlock (AGENTS.md Rule 8).

[DEC-M02] Jetson Orin Nano 40-Pin Header I2C Bus Mapping:
- Finding: The 40-pin header pins 3 and 5 correspond to Tegra controller
  c250000.i2c, registered in the Linux kernel as /dev/i2c-7.
- Pin Mapping:
    Pin 1: 3.3V Logic Power
    Pin 3: I2C1_SDA (Data)
    Pin 5: I2C1_SCL (Clock)
    Pin 6 / Pin 9: GND (System Ground)
- Bus Scan: i2cdetect -y -r 7 successfully detected:
    0x40: PCA9685 Primary Device Address
    0x70: PCA9685 Power-On Default All-Call Broadcast Address

[DEC-M03] Pin Order Discrepancy on PCA9685 Breakout vs Jetson:
- Finding: The PCA9685 6-pin input header sequence is:
    [GND, OE, SCL, SDA, VCC, V+]
  Notice that SCL is pin 3 and SDA is pin 4 on the breakout.
- On the Jetson 40-pin header:
    Pin 3 is SDA, and Pin 5 is SCL.
- Connecting pin 3 to pin 3 directly swaps SDA and SCL. Both lines will still
  measure ~3.0V due to pull-up resistors, but i2cdetect will return all '--'.
- Verification: Correcting the SDA/SCL swap with direct female-to-female jumper
  wires immediately brought 0x40 and 0x70 online on /dev/i2c-7.

[DEC-M04] TCA9548A Address Conflict vs Connection Guide Rev 1 (DEC-005):
- Context: Section 9A and Step 16 of VAPA_Robotic_Arm_Connection_Guide.pdf
  originally specified connecting TCA9548A A0, A1, A2 all to GND (address 0x70).
- Finding: Because the PCA9685 boots with the All-Call address 0x70 enabled in
  hardware, placing the TCA9548A at 0x70 causes an immediate address collision.
  Both chips respond to 0x70 simultaneously, corrupting multiplexer switching.
- Decision:
  * Primary Path (Recommended / DEC-005): Separate pin A0 from A1/A2 and pull
    A0 to 3.3V (setting TCA9548A address to 0x71). Pins A1 and A2 remain at GND.
  * Safety Warning: Never tie 3.3V to A0 while A0 is bridged to A1/A2. Doing so
    creates a direct 3.3V-to-GND dead short!
  * Fallback Path (Software Workaround): If desoldering is impractical on bench,
    the PCA9685 driver disables All-Call in MODE1, and config/hardware.yaml is
    updated to address 112 (0x70).

[DEC-M05] Soldered Main Board Diagnosis & Isolation:
- Finding: Direct F-F jumpers from Jetson Pins 3/5 to the PCA9685 detect cleanly.
  Routing through the soldered main board failed to detect any device.
- Root Cause Checklist for Bench Technician:
    1. Mirrored soldering: Perfboard bottom-side soldering mirrors left/right,
       frequently swapping SDA and SCL on socket headers.
    2. Solder whiskers/bridges between adjacent SDA and SCL socket pads.
    3. Bus clamping: Another unpowered or improperly oriented module (e.g.,
       TCA9548A, ADS1115, ESP32) pulling SDA/SCL to ground via ESD protection diodes.

4. TCA9548A MULTIPLEXER RESOLUTION & ADDRESSING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[DEC-M10] Solder-Bridged TCA9548A A0/A1/A2 Handling (Hardware vs Software Paths):
- Bench Finding: Operator confirmed that pins A0, A1, and A2 on the physical
  TCA9548A breakout board are soldered together and pulled to ground.
- Conflict Mechanism: When A0, A1, and A2 are grounded, TCA9548A selects I2C
  address 0x70. Because the PCA9685 power-on default hardware configuration has
  the All-Call broadcast address 0x70 enabled in MODE1, both chips acknowledge
  address 0x70 simultaneously. This results in bus contention, garbled ACK bits,
  and failure to route sensor channels.
- Resolution Options:
  * Primary Path (Recommended / DEC-005):
    1. Mechanically desolder or sever the bridge between A0 and A1/A2 using solder
       wick or a precision hobby knife.
    2. DMM Verification: With power off, verify open circuit (> 100 kΩ) between
       pin A0 and GND.
    3. Connect pin A0 to 3.3V logic power (VCC). Leave A1 and A2 tied to GND.
    4. CRITICAL SAFETY WARNING: Under NO circumstances apply 3.3V directly to A0
       while A0 is still bridged to A1/A2. Doing so creates a direct dead short
       from the 3.3V rail to GND, risking destruction of the Jetson's power rail!
    5. Outcome: TCA9548A moves permanently to address 0x71 (113 decimal).
  * Fallback Path (Software Workaround without Desoldering):
    1. In config/hardware.yaml, set tca9548a.address to 112 (0x70):
         jetson:
           tca9548a:
             address: 112
    2. Enforce Strict Initialization Sequencing: The PCA9685 driver MUST be
       initialized first at address 0x40 to clear MODE1 bit 0 (ALLCALL disabled).
       Once All-Call is cleared, PCA9685 unhooks from 0x70, allowing subsequent
       access to TCA9548A at 0x70 without collision.
    3. Tooling Compatibility: tools/calibration/calibrate_encoders.py and
       tools/bench/test_encoders.py have been updated to dynamically query
       config/hardware.yaml so both 0x71 and 0x70 work transparently.

[DEC-M11] TCA9548A Multiplexer Downstream Topology & AS5600 Encoders:
- Downstream Channel Topology:
    Channel 0: Finger Group AS5600 Magnetic Rotary Encoder (Address 0x36)
    Channel 1: Wrist Flexion/Extension AS5600 Magnetic Rotary Encoder (Address 0x36)
    Channel 2: Wrist Pronation/Supination AS5600 Magnetic Rotary Encoder (Address 0x36)
    Channel 3: Forearm Rotation AS5600 Magnetic Rotary Encoder (Address 0x36)
    Channels 4-7: Reserved / Unused
- Mux Switching Safety Invariant: The TCA9548A control register must only have
  at most one channel bit enabled at any time (1 << ch). Enabling multiple
  channels simultaneously shorts downstream I2C sub-buses together, causing
  address collisions between the four identical 0x36 AS5600 encoders.

[DEC-M18] TCA9548A /RESET Pin Float Behavior (1.749V) & Address Pin Grounding:
- Bench Observation: Multimeter measurement revealed the TCA9548A /RESET pin sitting
  at 1.749V while connected to the system.
- Root Cause: On 3.3V CMOS logic, an unconnected input floats near half-rail (~1.65V
  to 1.75V) due to internal ESD protection diode balance. The TCA9548A /RESET pin is
  active-LOW with a minimum input HIGH threshold (V_IH) of 0.7 * VCC = 2.31V.
  At 1.749V (< 2.31V), the internal reset circuitry remains asserted or in an
  indeterminate state, completely disabling the I2C interface so the chip never
  sends an ACK to 0x71 or 0x70.
- Mandatory Action: /RESET MUST be pulled solidly to 3.3V (VCC) via a direct tie
  or a 10 kΩ pull-up resistor, bringing its voltage to 3.30V.
- Address Pin Grounding Rule (A1 & A2): Do NOT disconnect A1 and A2 from ground!
  High-impedance CMOS address lines must never be left floating. Floating address
  lines pick up RF noise, oscillate, and cause the I2C address to jump randomly
  between 0x70, 0x71, 0x73, and 0x77. For reliable address 0x71, A0 must be tied
  to 3.3V, and A1 and A2 MUST remain solidly tied to GND.

[DEC-M19] Connection Guide Rev 1 Omission of TCA9548A /RESET vs Breakout Clones:
- Discrepancy: Section 9A of VAPA_Robotic_Arm_Connection_Guide.pdf lists VCC, GND,
  SDA, SCL, A0, A1, A2, and channel pins, but omits the /RESET pin entirely.
- Cause: The guide assumed an official Adafruit TCA9548A breakout board, which has
  an onboard 10 kΩ pull-up resistor pre-soldered between /RESET and VIN. On Adafruit
  boards, leaving /RESET disconnected works because the onboard resistor automatically
  pulls it to 3.3V.
- Clone Board Hazard: Generic / clone breakout boards (e.g. purple CJMCU modules)
  often omit the onboard pull-up resistor or leave the footprint unpopulated.
  Consequently, leaving /RESET disconnected leaves it floating at 1.749V, shutting
  down the multiplexer.
- 3.3V Rail Sharing Rule: It is 100% electrically safe and standard engineering practice
  to connect /RESET to the exact same 3.3V rail/wire where A0 and VCC are connected.
  Both A0 and /RESET are high-impedance CMOS inputs drawing < 1 µA of leakage current.
  The connection can be made either by wiring /RESET to Jetson 3.3V alongside A0, or
  by placing a small solder jumper directly between the /RESET and VCC header pins
  on the TCA9548A module.

[DEC-M20] Diagnostic Protocol for Total I2C Inactivity Following /RESET Pull-Up:
- Bench Observation: After connecting the TCA9548A /RESET pin to the Jetson 3.3V rail,
  executing 'i2cdetect -y -r 7' returned all null values (all '--' from 0x03 to 0x77).
- Low-Level Kernel Analysis: Direct SMBus byte probing across target addresses (0x40,
  0x70, 0x71) confirmed OSError [Errno 121] Remote I/O error (standard NACK).
  SCL is pulsing normally and SDA is pulled HIGH, but no device on the bus is pulling
  SDA LOW during the 9th clock pulse to acknowledge.
- Root Cause Differential:
    1. Common Ground Disconnection: GND jumper between Jetson (Pin 6/9) and the module
       came loose during rewiring. Without a common 0V reference, the chip cannot pull
       SDA low relative to Jetson GND, causing all addresses to return NACK.
    2. Reset Pin Voltage Ineffective: If the wire has a cold joint or is plugged into
       an inactive header pin, /RESET may still be floating at 1.75V or grounded (< 2.31V).
    3. 3.3V Power Rail Clamping / Short: If 3.3V was accidentally connected to a pin
       bridged to GND (such as A0 while still soldered to A1/A2/GND), the Jetson 3.3V
       rail drops to 0V, unpowering all I2C logic.
    4. Upstream vs Downstream Port Inversion: Jumper wires mistakenly connected to
       downstream channel pins (SD0/SC0) instead of main upstream SDA/SCL. Because all
       channels boot disabled, upstream remains completely silent.
    5. Pin Displacement / Offset: Breakout pin sequence [VIN, GND, SDA, SCL, RST, A0, A1, A2]
       has RST immediately adjacent to SCL and A0; inserting the wire may have shifted
       SCL or SDA off by one pin.
- Systematic 4-Point DMM Triage Protocol:
    Step 1 (With Power ON): Measure DC voltage from Jetson Pin 6 (GND) to:
      * Jetson Pin 1 (3.3V Rail): Must read 3.25V – 3.33V (confirms 3.3V rail is alive).
      * TCA9548A VCC Pin: Must read 3.25V – 3.33V (confirms chip has power).
      * TCA9548A /RESET Pin: Must read 3.25V – 3.33V (must be > 2.31V V_IH threshold).
      * TCA9548A SDA & SCL: Must read ~3.3V idle.
    Step 2 (With Power OFF): Measure continuity (Ohms) from Jetson Pin 6 (GND) to:
      * TCA9548A GND Pin: Must read < 1.0 Ω (confirms solid ground return).
    Step 3 (Isolation Check): Temporarily disconnect ONLY the new /RESET wire.
      * If PCA9685 (0x40) reappears, the /RESET wire was touching a shorted node or
        clamping the rail.
      * If all '--' persists, inspect physical seating of common GND and SDA/SCL.

[DEC-M26] Flying-Lead TCA9548A Multiplexer Re-Integration & Dual-Device Coexistence:
- Objective: Re-integrate TCA9548A multiplexer onto Jetson I2C Bus 7 in complete mid-air
  isolation (bypassing the damaged soldered board) alongside the verified PCA9685.
- 6-Pin Flying Wire Topology for TCA9548A:
    1. Jetson Pin 17 (3.3V Aux) --> TCA9548A Pin 1 (VIN)
    2. Jetson Pin 9 (GND Aux)   --> TCA9548A Pin 2 (GND)
    3. Jetson Pin 3 (SDA)       --> TCA9548A Pin 3 (SDA) [Paralleled with PCA9685 Pin 4]
    4. Jetson Pin 5 (SCL)       --> TCA9548A Pin 4 (SCL) [Paralleled with PCA9685 Pin 3]
    5. TCA9548A Pin 5 (/RESET)  --> Jumpered to Pin 1 (VIN / 3.3V) [Mandatory for clone boards, DEC-M18]
    6. TCA9548A Pin 6 (A0)      --> Jumpered to Pin 1 (VIN / 3.3V) [Sets Address to 0x71, DEC-005]
    7. TCA9548A Pins 7 & 8 (A1, A2) --> Jumpered to Pin 2 (GND) [Solid low reference]
- Bus Coexistence Validation:
    * Run 'i2cdetect -y -r 7'.
    * Expected Output:
        - 0x40: PCA9685 PWM Controller
        - 0x71: TCA9548A I2C Multiplexer
    * Pass Criteria: Both devices ACK reliably across 10 consecutive scans without bus lockup
      or clock stretching errors. Address 0x70 remains suppressed (DEC-M25).

[DEC-M27] Downstream AS5600 Magnetic Rotary Encoder Bus & Magnet Alignment Diagnostics:
- Downstream Channel Routing (TCA9548A Channels 0..3):
    * Channel 0: Finger Group AS5600 (tendon capstan spool angle)
    * Channel 1: Wrist Flexion/Extension AS5600
    * Channel 2: Wrist Pronation/Supination AS5600
    * Channel 3: Forearm Rotation AS5600
- Channel Switching Safety Invariant:
    * The control register (written to 0x71) must contain strictly a 1-hot bitmask (1 << ch).
    * Writing 0x00 deselects all channels immediately to isolate downstream branches.
- AS5600 Magnetic Alignment Status Register (Address 0x36, Register 0x0B):
    * Bit 5 (MD - Magnet Detected): Must be 1.
    * Bit 4 (ML - Magnet too Low): Must be 0 (if 1, magnet is too far away; air gap > 2.0 mm).
    * Bit 3 (MH - Magnet too High): Must be 0 (if 1, magnet is too close; air gap < 0.5 mm).
- Interactive Verification Tool:
    * Human operator command: python3 tools/bench/test_encoders.py
    * Interactive readout prints angle in degrees, MD status, and automatic gain control (AGC)
      value (register 0x1A, optimal range 64..192 counts).
    * Calibration offsets (zero mechanical position) are saved to config/hardware.yaml
      via tools/calibration/calibrate_encoders.py.

[DEC-M13] Multi-Module Subtraction & Bus Clamping Isolation:
- Root Cause Analysis: Direct jumpers confirmed the PCA9685 IC is functional.
  Failure on the soldered board typically stems from:
    1. Bottom-Side Perfboard Pin Mirroring: Viewing female header sockets from
       underneath reverses left/right pin order, frequently swapping SDA and SCL.
    2. Unpowered Module Clamping: If another module (e.g. TCA9548A, ADS1115, or
       ESP32) is seated in a socket with an open or unpowered VCC pin, the chip's
       internal ESD protection diodes will clamp SDA and SCL to ~0.5V to 0.7V,
       preventing any I2C communication across the entire bus.
- Progressive Subtraction Isolation Protocol:
    1. Power down and remove all IC modules from sockets (PCA9685, TCA9548A, ADS1115).
    2. Power on Jetson and measure voltage at Pin 3 (SDA) and Pin 5 (SCL):
       both must read 3.2V to 3.3V (pull-up resistors healthy).
    3. Plug in ONLY PCA9685 into its socket. Run 'i2cdetect -y -r 7'.
       Expected: 0x40 and 0x70 appear.
    4. If Step 3 succeeds, power down and insert TCA9548A. Power on and re-scan.
       Expected: 0x40 and 0x71 (or 0x70).
    5. If the bus locks up after inserting any module, that specific socket has
       an inverted pinout, missing VCC rail, or defective IC.

[DEC-M21] Soldered Board I2C Inversion & Routing Discontinuity Confirmation:
- Bench Observations & Elimination Findings:
    1. Multimeter confirmed /RESET pin reads 3.30V when tied to Jetson 3.3V rail.
    2. Continuity test between module GND and Jetson Pin 6 verified solid at < 1.0 Ω.
    3. Disconnecting /RESET produced identical scan output (all '--' on /dev/i2c-7).
- Invariant Deduction:
    * The /RESET pin was NOT the root cause of the silent bus. The 3.30V reading confirmed
      the reset circuit was satisfied.
    * Because PCA9685 previously verified fully operational at 0x40 and 0x70 when connected
      via direct female-to-female jumpers (DEC-M03 / DEC-M12), the failure of BOTH PCA9685
      and TCA9548A when routed through the soldered board isolates the defect to the
      physical board's I2C signal lines (SDA/SCL).
- Critical Pinout Inversion Between PCA9685 and TCA9548A Breakouts:
    * PCA9685 6-pin Header: [GND, OE, SCL, SDA, VCC, V+]  --> Pin 3 is SCL, Pin 4 is SDA.
    * TCA9548A 8-pin Header: [VIN, GND, SDA, SCL, RST, A0, A1, A2] --> Pin 3 is SDA, Pin 4 is SCL.
    * Jetson 40-pin Header: Pin 3 is SDA, Pin 5 is SCL.
    * Notice: SCL and SDA are physically INVERTED between the two breakout boards. If the
      soldered board routed pin 3 to pin 3 and pin 4 to pin 4 between sockets, or mirrored
      pins during bottom-side perfboard soldering, SDA and SCL are crossed.
- Mandatory Actionable DMM Diagnostic Protocol:
    1. Short Test (Power OFF): Measure resistance between SDA and SCL on the board.
       Target: > 10 kΩ (OL). If < 10 Ω, adjacent pads are bridged with solder.
    2. End-to-End Trace Mapping (Power OFF):
       - Jetson Pin 3 (SDA) -> PCA9685 Pin 4 (SDA) AND TCA9548A Pin 3 (SDA).
       - Jetson Pin 5 (SCL) -> PCA9685 Pin 3 (SCL) AND TCA9548A Pin 4 (SCL).
    3. Direct Jumper Bypass Test: Connect TCA9548A directly to Jetson Pins 3 & 5 using
       jumper wires (bypassing the soldered board). Both 0x71 and 0x40 will be confirmed.

[DEC-M22] Isolation of PCA9685 & Residual Perfboard Wire Stub Hazards:
- Bench Action: Operator cut all wiring to the TCA9548A multiplexer module to
  isolate the PCA9685 PWM driver directly to the Jetson I2C bus (Pins 3 & 5).
  Cut wire stubs remain soldered to the perfboard.
- Critical Electrical Hazards & Mitigations:
    1. Short-Circuit Hazard on Perfboard Stubs: Exposed copper strands on cut
       wire stubs can easily bridge adjacent pads, 3.3V/5V/6V power rails, or GND.
       A short between 3.3V and GND will collapse the Jetson power rail.
       * Mandatory Action: Trim all wire stubs flush with the perfboard, desolder
         them, or insulate each exposed end with heat shrink or electrical tape.
    2. Parallel Trace Interference: If the PCA9685 remains plugged into its
       perfboard socket while flying jumper leads are attached, any existing
       short or solder bridge on the perfboard traces remains in parallel with
       the bus lines.
       * Recommendation: Remove the PCA9685 breakout entirely from the perfboard
         socket and connect it purely via flying jumper wires in mid-air.
    3. Mandatory 4-Wire Connection for PCA9685:
       Connecting ONLY SDA and SCL is insufficient; common GND and VCC logic
       power are mandatory:
         * Jetson Pin 1 (3.3V)   --> PCA9685 Pin 5 (VCC)
         * Jetson Pin 6/9 (GND)  --> PCA9685 Pin 1 (GND)
         * Jetson Pin 3 (SDA)    --> PCA9685 Pin 4 (SDA)
         * Jetson Pin 5 (SCL)    --> PCA9685 Pin 3 (SCL)
    4. Expected Bus Response: With TCA9548A removed and PCA9685 wired cleanly,
       'i2cdetect -y -r 7' should immediately detect 0x40 (and 0x70).

[DEC-M23] PCA9685 Standalone Power Failure Triage & Jetson 3.3V Rail Recovery:
- Bench Observation: Connecting the PCA9685 breakout board directly via jumper
  wires resulted in zero power (onboard PWR LED remains unlit).
- Root Cause Differential:
    1. VCC vs V+ Pin Confusion: The PCA9685 breakout board's onboard green Power
       LED and logic circuitry are powered strictly from Pin 5 (VCC). The green
       screw terminal and Pin 6 (V+) power only the servos, not the chip logic.
       Connecting 3.3V to V+ leaves the chip unpowered.
    2. Jetson PMIC 3.3V Rail Overcurrent Trip: If exposed perfboard wire stubs
       shorted 3.3V to GND during wire cutting, the Tegra PMIC shuts off the
       3.3V rail. Voltage between Pin 1 and Pin 6 drops to 0V.
       * Recovery: Full power cycle (unplug barrel jack for 15s) resets PMIC latch.
    3. Dual 3.3V Pin Redundancy: If Jetson Pin 1 was damaged or tripped, Pin 17
       is an independent 3.3V logic supply on the 40-pin header.
    4. 40-Pin Header Zigzag Counting Error: Pin 1 and Pin 3 are on Row 1 (inner),
       while Pin 2, 4, 6 are on Row 2 (outer). Linear row-wise counting connects
       to incorrect pins (e.g. Pin 3 instead of Pin 2, Pin 5 instead of Pin 3).

[DEC-M24] PCA9685 Standalone 3.3V Power Restoration & Bus Recognition (0x40 & 0x70):
- Bench Observation & Triage:
    * Follow-up to DEC-M23: Multimeter probe across Jetson Pin 1 (3.3V) and Pin 6 (GND).
    * If 0.0V measured: Confirmed Tegra PMIC short-circuit shutdown latch caused by
      exposed perfboard wire-cutting stubs.
    * Latch Clearing Action: Disconnect 19V DC barrel jack power to Jetson for 15-20 seconds
      to allow internal bulk capacitors to fully discharge. Re-insert power barrel jack and
      allow JetPack to boot. Confirm Pin 1 reads 3.30V ± 0.05V with DMM.
    * Header Zigzag Count Verification:
      Inner Row 1 (pins closer to SoC heatsink):
        Pin 1 (3.3V), Pin 3 (I2C1_SDA), Pin 5 (I2C1_SCL), Pin 7 (GPIO), Pin 9 (GND), Pin 17 (3.3V Aux).
      Outer Row 2 (pins closer to board edge):
        Pin 2 (5.0V), Pin 4 (5.0V), Pin 6 (GND), Pin 8 (UART TX), Pin 10 (UART RX).
- Dedicated 4-Wire Flying Jumper Connections (Mid-Air Isolation):
    1. Jetson Pin 1 (3.3V)   --> PCA9685 Pin 5 (VCC) [Confirm Onboard Green PWR LED lights up].
    2. Jetson Pin 6 (GND)    --> PCA9685 Pin 1 (GND).
    3. Jetson Pin 3 (SDA)    --> PCA9685 Pin 4 (SDA).
    4. Jetson Pin 5 (SCL)    --> PCA9685 Pin 3 (SCL).
    * Note: PCA9685 Pin 2 (/OE) and Pin 6 (V+) are left DISCONNECTED during initial bus scan.
- Confirmed Bus Recognition:
    * Run 'i2cdetect -y -r 7'.
    * Expected Output:
        - Address 0x40: PCA9685 PWM Driver active.
        - Address 0x70: PCA9685 Default Power-On All-Call Address active.

[DEC-M25] PCA9685 All-Call Address Suppression & Register Verification:
- Problem: The power-on default All-Call broadcast address 0x70 conflicts with TCA9548A
  if its address pins are pulled to ground.
- Root Cause: PCA9685 register 0x00 (MODE1) powers on with bit 0 (ALLCALL) set to 1.
- Invariant & Mitigation:
    * The VAPA PCA9685 driver ('vapa/hal/pca9685.py') explicitly suppresses All-Call during
      initialization when 'disable_allcall: true' is configured in config/hardware.yaml.
    * Manual Register Clear Command (via SMBus / i2c-tools):
        i2cset -y 7 0x40 0x00 0x00
      Writing 0x00 to MODE1 clears ALLCALL, SLEEP, and restart bits, disabling 0x70.
    * Bus Scan Re-check: 'i2cdetect -y -r 7' now shows strictly 0x40; address 0x70 disappears.

[DEC-M30] Bus 1 (c240000.i2c) INA3221 / FUSB301 False Positive & Header Pin 27/28 (ID_I2C) Trap:
- Bench Observation: Operator moved SDA to Pin 27 and SCL to Pin 28, then executed
  'sudo i2cdetect -y -r 1'. Scan returned:
    Address 0x25: 'UU'
    Address 0x40: 'UU'
    All other addresses: '--'
- In-Depth Architecture Analysis & Root Cause:
    1. 'UU' Does NOT Mean PCA9685:
       The symbol 'UU' indicates that an address is actively claimed and reserved
       by a Linux kernel device driver. An unconfigured external I2C device always
       reports numeric hex (e.g. '40'), NEVER 'UU'.
    2. Bus 1 (c240000.i2c) is Strictly Internal:
       On the NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano, /dev/i2c-1 is an internal system monitoring bus
       soldered directly on the module/carrier board:
         - 0x40 ('UU') is the Texas Instruments INA3221 triple-channel power/current
           monitor IC (/sys/bus/i2c/devices/1-0040, driver 'ina3221').
         - 0x25 ('UU') is the ON Semiconductor FUSB301 USB Type-C controller
           (/sys/bus/i2c/devices/1-0025, driver 'fusb301').
       This bus does NOT connect to external expansion header pins.
    3. Pin 27 and Pin 28 Functionality:
       On the Jetson 40-pin header, Pin 27 (ID_SD) and Pin 28 (ID_SC) connect to
       controller 3160000.i2c, registered in Linux as /dev/i2c-0. This bus is dedicated
       to the HAT ID EEPROM (addresses 0x50 and 0x57) and lacks standard pull-up
       resistors for general peripherals.
    4. Mandatory User I2C Bus Mapping:
       The general-purpose user I2C bus on the Jetson Orin Nano 40-pin header is
       strictly:
         - Pin 3: I2C1_SDA (Data)
         - Pin 5: I2C1_SCL (Clock)
       This controller is c250000.i2c, registered in Linux as /dev/i2c-7.
- Resolution & Next Action:
    1. Move SDA and SCL jumpers back to Jetson Pin 3 (SDA) and Pin 5 (SCL).
    2. Ensure PCA9685 onboard green PWR LED is illuminated by supplying 3.3V to Pin 5 (VCC).
    3. Execute scan strictly on bus 7: 'i2cdetect -y -r 7'.

[DEC-M31] Silkscreen Name vs Header Pin Number Transposition Hazard & LED Power Triage:
- Bench Observation: 'i2cdetect -y -r 7' returned all null values ('--' from 0x03 to 0x77).
  Low-level SMBus probe confirmed OSError [Errno 121] Remote I/O error (standard NACK).
  The Jetson Tegra I2C master controller is fully functional, generating start/clock cycles,
  but receives zero ACK from the bus.
- Critical Failure Mechanisms & Root Causes:
    1. Pin Number 1:1 Transposition Hazard:
       Connecting by pin index number (Jetson Pin N to PCA9685 Pin N) causes a catastrophic
       signal swap:
         - Jetson Pin 1 (3.3V) --> PCA9685 Pin 1 (GND) [Dead short across 3.3V power rail!]
         - Jetson Pin 3 (SDA)  --> PCA9685 Pin 3 (SCL) [SDA and SCL swapped]
         - Jetson Pin 5 (SCL)  --> PCA9685 Pin 5 (VCC) [Clock line shorted to power rail]
         - Jetson Pin 6 (GND)  --> PCA9685 Pin 6 (V+)  [Ground tied to servo power]
    2. Silkscreen-Strict Label Rule:
       WIRING MUST BE PERFORMED STRICTLY BY PRINTED SILKSCREEN TEXT LABELS, NEVER PIN INDICES:
         * Jetson Pin 1 (3.3V)  --> PCA9685 Pin labeled 'VCC' (Pin 5)
         * Jetson Pin 9 (GND)   --> PCA9685 Pin labeled 'GND' (Pin 1)
         * Jetson Pin 3 (SDA)   --> PCA9685 Pin labeled 'SDA' (Pin 4)
         * Jetson Pin 5 (SCL)   --> PCA9685 Pin labeled 'SCL' (Pin 3)
    3. All-Inner-Row Connection Rule (Eliminates Row-Hop Errors):
       Jetson Pin 1 (3.3V), Pin 3 (SDA), Pin 5 (SCL), and Pin 9 (GND) are ALL on the
       exact same INNER ROW (Row 1, nearest the SoC heatsink). By using Pin 9 instead
       of Pin 6 for ground, the operator avoids jumping between inner and outer rows.
    4. Onboard Green PWR LED Invariant:
       The PCA9685 breakout board has a green SMD PWR LED wired directly across VCC and GND.
       If this LED is OFF, the chip has zero logic power, and no I2C communication is possible.
       The LED must be illuminated before running any software or bus scan.
    5. Complete Perfboard Mechanical Isolation:
       The PCA9685 module must be physically pulled out of any socket or perfboard and held
       in mid-air via 4 flying jumper wires to guarantee no residual solder whiskers or
       shorted traces interfere with the bus.

[DEC-M32] Total Bus Inactivity Triage: Master vs Slave Hardware Elimination Protocol:

6. ACTUATOR POWER & HARDWARE SAFETY INTERLOCK GATING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[DEC-M14] Dual-Rail Power Architecture & Star Ground Reference:
- Power Rail Separation:
    * 3.3V Logic Rail: Sourced strictly from Jetson Pin 1 (3.3V, max 500 mA).
      Powers PCA9685 VCC, TCA9548A VCC, and AS5600 encoders.
    * 6.0V Actuator Rail: Sourced strictly from high-current bench DC supply
      or 2S LiFePO4 battery (up to 20A peak). Connected to PCA9685 V+ and GND
      screw terminals via physical E-Stop contactor / BTS50015 PROFET.
    * MANDATORY SAFETY RULE: NEVER connect servo power (V+) to Jetson 5V or 3.3V
      pins. Servo stall currents (2.5A per motor, up to 22.5A peak across 9 servos)
      will vaporize Jetson carrier board traces and cause severe hardware damage.
- Star Ground Bonding: The GND of the 6.0V servo supply, ESP32 GND, and Jetson
  Pin 6 GND must meet at a single, low-impedance common star ground point on
  the main board to ensure an identical voltage reference for PWM signals and
  prevent ground bounce.

[DEC-M15] ESP32 Output Enable (/OE) Safety Supervisor Bringup:
- Active-LOW Hardware Output Gate:
    * PCA9685 /OE (Pin 23) gates all 16 PWM output stages in hardware.
    * Power-on Default: ESP32 GPIO25 boots in input/pull-up state, holding /OE
      at 3.3V (HIGH / Disabled). Servos receive 0% duty cycle and remain completely
      limp and backdriveable by hand without mechanical resistance.
    * Arming Transition: Only after explicit operator command and all pre-flight
      checks pass does ESP32 drive GPIO25 LOW (0V / Enabled).
- Verification Procedure:
    * Execution: python3 tools/bench/test_oe.py --channel 0
    * Multimeter Check: Measure voltage between PCA9685 /OE and GND:
        - In DISARMED state: 3.25V to 3.33V (HIGH).
        - In ARMED state: < 0.1V (LOW).

[DEC-M28] Actuator Rail 6.0V Bringup & ESP32 /OE Safety Supervisor Interlock:
- Hardware Non-Negotiables Before Applying Servo Power:
    1. Mechanical Tendon Disconnection: All nylon/Dyneema tendon lines must be completely
       unhooked from servo horns. Servos must rotate completely free of mechanical load.
    2. Physical Emergency Stop in Hand: Industrial latching mushroom switch wired in series
       with the BTS50015 PROFET solid-state high-side switch gating the 6.0V rail.
    3. Bench Power Supply Current Limit: Set bench DC power supply to 6.0V with current limit
       set conservatively to 2.0A for single-joint bringup (preventing damage in case of stall).
- Interlock Verification Protocol:
    1. Phase 1 (Disarmed Baseline):
       * Power up system with ESP32 flashed and Jetson running.
       * Multimeter check: Measure voltage between PCA9685 /OE (Pin 2) and GND.
         Must read 3.25V to 3.33V (HIGH / Disabled).
       * Move servo horns gently by hand: Servos must be completely limp with 0 holding torque.
    2. Phase 2 (ESP32 Arming Test):
       * Run 'python3 tools/bench/test_oe.py --channel 0' (human-confirmed bench script).
       * Multimeter check: /OE drops to < 0.1V (LOW / Enabled).
       * Servo on Channel 0 engages neutral hold position (1500 µs).
    3. Phase 3 (E-Stop Trigger Test):
       * While armed, hit physical E-Stop button.
       * ESP32 immediately de-asserts GPIO25 (driving /OE HIGH) and PROFET cuts 6V power.
       * Servo immediately releases hold and returns to limp state in < 1 ms.
